Your aunt, the astronomer, left you three things in her will: her old observatory on Lantern Hill, a note reading "Watch for the comet tonight," and absolutely no instructions.

The comet passes at midnight. The telescope, you are told, has not worked in years.

Sixty-one years ago I sat on this hill with my own aunt and watched the Lantern Comet come up out of the east, and I promised I would see it again. I may not manage it. You will have to see it for both of us.
