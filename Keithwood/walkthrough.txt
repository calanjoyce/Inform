Keithwood: front yard demo walkthrough
======================================

There's no puzzle and no ending yet. The demo is two locations, the Front Yard
and the Gravel Strip to its north, for trying out the time shift. Four things
that are gone have left traces:

  - tire marks on the driveway                       -> your dad's car
  - a slight mound in the lawn, north of the shrub   -> the old climbing tree
  - tire tracks in the gravel strip (go north)       -> your mom's car
  - a small square hole at the strip's west end      -> the mailbox where it
                                                        used to stand

The mailbox today stands beside the driveway in the Front Yard. Open it.

LOOK CLOSELY AT, TOUCH or SEARCH a trace (or the gravel, driveway or lawn) to
bring back what made it. EXAMINE it more than once: the cars and the tree shift
each look to another of the ways they might have been. REMEMBER a thing to hear
what it meant. Leave it alone for eight turns and it fades. Bring all four back
at once and the whole yard slips into the past for a moment.

Which car or tree appears is random, so your own playthrough will differ from
the sample below.

Sample playthrough
------------------





You haven't stood in this yard in years. The house looks smaller than you remember, and different: the colors, the plantings. But some things leave marks.

(This is a short demo of the front of the house. Things that are gone have left traces here. LOOK CLOSELY AT them, TOUCH them or SEARCH them, and see what comes back. Type HINT for help.)

Front Yard
The lawn slopes gently up from Keithwood Drive toward the house, a split-level of red brick and cream siding. To your left, a low brick wing holds the garage, with a wide picture window set above its door, and a gravel driveway slopes down to it from the road, for the garage door sits a little lower than the front patio. To your right rises the two-story part of the house: dark red shutters frame the upstairs windows, and a brick chimney climbs the far end. Directly in front of you, a small concrete patio leads past a weeping shrub to the front door. A mailbox stands right beside the driveway on its eastern side. Two enormous old trees shade the lawn to the west, and down at the bottom of the lawn a strip of gravel runs along the road.

>x mailbox
A mailbox on a post, right beside the driveway on its eastern side. Its little flag is down. It is closed.

>open mailbox
You open the mailbox. You half expect something to be waiting inside for you, the way something always is in the stories. It's empty.

>x mound
A slight rise in the lawn, a few feet north of the weeping shrub. The grass over it is a little greener than the rest. You'd have to look more closely to see more.

>look closely at mound
You crouch for a closer look at the slight mound in the lawn.

The grass over the mound stirs. Then there's a trunk where there was only lawn, and branches spreading overhead: the crabapple tree that stood a few feet from the weeping shrub. The one you used to climb.

>x tree
(the crabapple tree)
A slim birch, its white bark peeling in papery curls, its lower branches just within reach. Wasn't it a crabapple tree a moment ago?

>x tree
(the birch tree)
A crabapple, low and twisty, with branches that seem made for climbing. Wasn't it a birch tree a moment ago?

>remember tree
(the crabapple tree)
You climbed it once. A branch broke, and you fell. You remember the falling better than the tree.

>climb tree
(the crabapple tree)
You reach for the lowest branch, and your stomach drops the way it did the day one broke under you. You let go.

>search driveway
You crouch for a closer look at the tire marks on the driveway.

Something catches the light at the bottom of the driveway. Your dad's tan Volkswagen Rabbit is parked down by the garage, as if it had never left.

>x dad's car
A blue Honda Accord hatchback. Or was it green? It's blue now, anyway. Wasn't it a tan Volkswagen Rabbit a moment ago?

>x dad's car
A green Honda Accord hatchback. Or was it blue? It's green now, anyway. Wasn't it a blue Honda Accord a moment ago?

Gravel Strip
A narrow strip of gravel runs between Keithwood Drive and the bottom of the lawn, just wide enough for one car. The lawn slopes up to the south toward the house.

Up at the house, your dad's green Honda Accord sits in the driveway.

Up on the lawn, a crabapple tree stands a few feet north of the weeping shrub.

>x gravel
Gravel along the edge of the road, wide enough for one car. Tire tracks are pressed into it, and at the west end there's a small square hole in the ground, half grown over.

>touch tracks in the gravel
You crouch and lay your hand on the tire tracks in the gravel.

The gravel crunches, though nobody has moved. When you look up, a car is parked on the strip along the road, right over its own tracks: your mom's green station wagon, the way it almost always was.

>x mom's car
A small silver Honda Fit, tidy and upright, parked neatly on the gravel. Wasn't it a green station wagon a moment ago?

>x mom's car
A tan Dodge Caravan, boxy and practical, with its sliding door on the far side. Wasn't it a silver Honda Fit a moment ago?

>remember mom's car
Your mom's car, out here on the gravel strip, almost always. Which car it was depends on the year, and the years have run together.

>x mailbox
The mailbox isn't down here anymore. It stands up by the driveway now, though the west end of the strip still remembers where it used to be.

>look closely at hole
You crouch for a closer look at the small square hole at the west end of the strip.

The grass around the hole parts. A post rises out of it, and on top of the post a mailbox: right here at the west end of the gravel strip, where it used to be, not over by the driveway where it stands now.

>x old mailbox
The mailbox on its post at the west end of the gravel strip, where it used to stand.

>remember old mailbox
The mailbox used to stand here, at the west end of the gravel strip. Now it stands over by the driveway.

>hint
Things that are gone have left traces in the yard: marks in the gravel, a hole at the west end of the gravel strip, marks on the driveway, a rise in the lawn. LOOK CLOSELY AT a trace, or TOUCH or SEARCH it, to bring back what made it.

What comes back is hazy. Look at it more than once and it may not be what it was. REMEMBER something to hear what it meant to you. Memories fade if you leave them alone for a while.
