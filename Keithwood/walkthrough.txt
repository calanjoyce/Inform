Keithwood: front yard demo walkthrough
======================================

There's no puzzle and no ending yet. The demo is one room for trying out the
time shift. Three things that are gone have left traces in the front yard:

  - tire tracks in the gravel strip along the road  -> your mom's car
  - tire marks on the driveway                       -> your dad's car
  - a slight mound in the lawn, north of the shrub   -> the old climbing tree

LOOK CLOSELY AT, TOUCH or SEARCH a trace (or the gravel, driveway or lawn) to
bring back what made it. EXAMINE it more than once: each look it shifts to
another of the ways it might have been. REMEMBER it to hear what it meant.
Leave it alone for eight turns and it fades. Bring all three back at once and
the whole yard slips into the past for a moment.

Which car or tree appears is random, so your own playthrough will differ from
the sample below.

Sample playthrough
------------------





You haven't stood in this yard in years. The house looks smaller than you remember, and different: the colors, the plantings. But some things leave marks.

A slight rise in the lawn, a few feet north of the weeping shrub. The grass over it is a little greener than the rest. You'd have to look more closely to see more.

>look closely at mound
You crouch for a closer look at the slight mound in the lawn.

The grass over the mound stirs. Then there's a trunk where there was only lawn, and branches spreading overhead: the maple tree that stood a few feet from the weeping shrub. The one you used to climb.

>x tree
(the maple tree)
A slim birch, its white bark peeling in papery curls, its lower branches just within reach. Wasn't it a maple tree a moment ago?

>x tree
(the birch tree)
A young maple, its trunk forking low, close enough to the ground for a kid to get a foot into. Wasn't it a birch tree a moment ago?

>remember tree
(the maple tree)
You climbed it once. A branch broke, and you fell. You remember the falling better than the tree.

>climb tree
(the maple tree)
You reach for the lowest branch, and your stomach drops the way it did the day one broke under you. You let go.

>x gravel
A strip of gravel along the edge of the road, wide enough for one car. Tire tracks are pressed into it.

>touch tracks in the gravel
You crouch and lay your hand on the tire tracks in the gravel.

The gravel crunches, though nobody has moved. When you look up, a car is parked on the strip along the road, right over its own tracks: your mom's tan minivan, the way it almost always was.

>x mom's car
A long green Dodge station wagon, its sides trimmed in wood paneling, its nose pointed down the road. Wasn't it a tan minivan a moment ago?

>x mom's car
A tan Dodge Caravan, boxy and practical, with its sliding door on the far side. Wasn't it a green station wagon a moment ago?

>remember mom's car
Your mom's car, out here on the gravel strip, almost always. Which car it was depends on the year, and the years have run together.

>search driveway
You crouch for a closer look at the tire marks on the driveway.

Something catches the light at the bottom of the driveway. Your dad's tan Volkswagen Rabbit is parked down by the garage, as if it had never left.

For a moment, all of it is here at once: your mom's tan minivan on the gravel, your dad's tan Volkswagen Rabbit in the driveway, the maple tree by the shrub. The yard is the way it was, or close enough.

>hint
Things that are gone have left traces in the yard: marks in the gravel, marks on the driveway, a rise in the lawn. LOOK CLOSELY AT a trace, or TOUCH or SEARCH it, to bring back what made it.

What comes back is hazy. Look at it more than once and it may not be what it was. REMEMBER something to hear what it meant to you. Memories fade if you leave them alone for a while.
